Exhibition Concept

Every Copy Takes Place explores how information is continually transformed, copied, recombined, and presented anew as it moves between physical and digital media. The exhibition considers the forms information takes in different media and how medium, material, and space shape its presentation and our experience of looking.

Read the exhibition concept

Physical

In the physical world, information depends on a tangible carrier such as paper, a photograph, a book, or a print. Each has dimensions, weight, texture, and a specific location. Every copy produces a new object and occupies new space. What can be preserved and how it circulates depend on the material, the way it is made, and the conditions around it.

Digital

Digital technologies encode information as data that computers can process. An image can be computed, taken apart, edited, duplicated, and recombined. The same information can appear at different resolutions and in different file formats and visual forms, and travel between devices and spaces through digital networks.

Hybrid

Digital information remains tied to the material world. Images depend on screens, computers, storage devices, and other physical media to appear and be perceived. Printing, projection, and related processes give digital information physical form, so it takes on a material, a scale, and a location again.

Through photography, print works, interactive digital installations, and moving images shown in space, the exhibition traces these transformations. Visitors can observe the forms physical and digital information take and experience how the same information produces new visual relationships through interaction, movement, and changes in viewpoint.

What happens to information when it passes from one medium to another? How much of what we perceive and understand is shaped by the medium, the material, and the space in which we encounter it?

Works & references

Floor Plan

Across two floors, physical and digital works share space. The model shows how three ways of presenting information change what can be seen and how it is encountered.

Floor 1 · Left of the entrance

Physical

The printing block, illuminated manuscript, and book spines on the left of the entrance give information a visible scale and a place. The case, wall, and shelves make the differences between a carved surface, a page, and a volume apparent.

Floor 1 · Right side

Digital

Images in Ruoxi Wu’s Becoming Data take shape within a computer game. In Rafael Lozano-Hemmer’s Please Empty Your Pockets, visitors place small objects on a conveyor belt. A scanner records them, and their digital images can reappear on the belt after the objects have been removed.

Atrium · Across both floors

Hybrid

Translucent panels carry printed portrait fragments into the atrium. Their separation, height, and overlap become part of the image. From the ground floor or along the upper railing, the fragments are seen in different relationships to each other and to the room.
